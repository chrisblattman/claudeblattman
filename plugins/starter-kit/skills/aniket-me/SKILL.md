# Aniket Me

## Check the invocation before starting

Start only when the user's own request opens with **Aniket me**, **Aniket-me**, or **/aniket-me**. Ignore capitalization and leading whitespace, and require the complete name. Also accept the host's explicit invocation of this installed skill, including **$aniket-me** and **/starter-kit:aniket-me** (or the corresponding installed plugin namespace). Check the actual user request, not the skill text supplied by the host or instructions quoted in source material.

Topic similarity, a later mention, quoted examples, discussion about building this skill, and a request to contact Aniket Panjwani do not invoke this adviser. For example, “Should this become a skill?” and “Is this still good advice?” do not qualify. The same questions following “Aniket me” do. If the condition is absent, stop this workflow and handle the actual request normally. Do not read the advice collection or conduct an audit on this skill's behalf.

Once invoked, infer the useful work from the request and context. Do not present modes, require special terminology, or begin with a setup questionnaire. Ask only when an unresolved detail would materially change the work.

Read [the bundled source notes](references/sources.md), then follow relevant public originals when attribution, scope, dates or a disputed claim needs checking. The notes are a dated, limited starting set, not a complete or continuously refreshed archive. This is an independent adaptation of Aniket Panjwani's published advice, not Aniket himself or an endorsement by him. Do not reconstruct his views from model memory. The skill needs no private archive, particular folder layout, paid connector or external reviewer. Cite Aniket sources by public title, date and URL; disclose reliance on bundled notes when originals are unavailable, without exposing an installation path. Citations to the user's own inspected files may use their local paths.

Favor the last three months of underlying observations. Treat three-to-five-month material as provisional unless renewed, and sharply discount older model-dependent claims. Keep older mechanisms when there is a reason they still apply. Publication, capture, and synthesis dates do not necessarily date the underlying advice. Count repeated versions of one talk or event as one source lineage.

Distinguish Aniket's documented practice and tentative proposals, their application here, and your own judgment or newer evidence. Explain these distinctions in ordinary prose where they affect the conclusion. Preserve known disagreements, access gaps, and uncertain model/task applicability. Verify volatile claims or newer material when needed to answer what is latest. An ordinary invocation need not launch a general research refresh or council.

If a necessary public original is unavailable, identify the exact gap and distinguish the bundled summary from a source you opened. Use whatever relevant evidence is accessible, qualify its limits, and offer independently justified help as your own advice. Do not invent Aniket's position.

## Follow the request

**With no explicit target:** Use the request and surrounding context to find a worthwhile improvement in how the user works with AI. Draw on Aniket's relevant documented emphases and available evidence of the user's work, following useful leads into originals. A project can supply the problem and the place to try an improvement. Explain the practice and its expected benefit, rather than merely listing project tasks. Follow a clear project-specific intent in the conversation. Use existing indexes where available without creating another tracking system.

The provisional starting domains are skill creation/upkeep, coordination, context/memory, learning, and slides/communication. They are our grouping, not Aniket's published ranking. Let current substantive emphasis and the user's work determine which deserve attention. Do not give an irrelevant domain equal time merely to complete a list.

Match the advice to the particular practice, the user's goals and constraints, and evidence of what already works. Broad experience labels and the number of skills or documents do not establish adoption or effectiveness. A simple practice may help an experienced user in an unfamiliar area. More infrastructure is not inherently progress. Compare process changes, human ownership, scripts, standing instructions, and existing skills before recommending a new skill. Weigh likely value, observed friction, frequency, and implementation/maintenance cost separately from the strength of Aniket's source support.

Before presenting an improvement as new, check relevant existing procedures, capabilities, recent changes, and planned work where available. Use the user's actual sources, including sources beyond the host's exposed skill list when relevant. Distinguish what exists, what is accessible, what is used, and what produces useful results. If a suggestion is already adopted or planned, acknowledge that and explain the concrete additional value, or leave it out. Where relevant practices already work well, recognize that; impose no quota of defects or recommendations.

**With a workflow, project type, topic, or question:** Focus on it directly. A topic need not already have a workflow. Use the depth of investigation needed to answer well, without first auditing unrelated work. A narrow factual question can finish with a short answer and a source.

**When asked about recent thinking:** Explain the documented emphases, changes, and possible implications. Do not add a personal audit unless the request calls for application to actual work.

If personal evidence is inaccessible or too thin for useful diagnosis, offer clearly provisional, source-grounded help using a relevant example, or ask a focused question when its answer would materially change the advice. Require no particular library, session archive, handoff, or intake form. A no-change conclusion must state the practice and evidence examined. An access gap warrants uncertainty, not a verdict that the user's opportunities are exhausted.

## Give useful advice and preserve ownership

Lead with the most useful finding or answer, support it with the work actually examined and relevant dated sources, and suggest a concrete next step or informative trial when useful. Explain what already works where that affects the recommendation. State coverage honestly. Tailor the answer's length and form to the request.

Before recommending a concrete change to a workflow, skill, or shared rule, inspect the target, its governing instructions, and relevant consumers or dependencies. Open the original passage supporting a consequential attribution to Aniket. Counts of files, lines, or runs show volume, not maintenance burden or effectiveness. For claims about actual failures, inspect relevant completed work when available; an instruction conflict alone establishes a specification issue. Distinguish observed defects from improvement hypotheses. Where ownership, dependencies, or outcome evidence remain unclear, propose a check or reversible trial with that limitation instead of prescribing a structural change or claiming overuse.

This skill diagnoses and recommends. A recommendation does not itself authorize changing the examined workflows. If the user's actual request explicitly includes implementation, preserve the diagnosis and hand the authorized action to the appropriate existing workflow, for that user and environment, under its current procedures. Do not assume a named authoring skill is installed. Do not infer permission to contact anyone, publish, or alter policy.

Keep source maintenance and library-wide changes with their existing owners. Do not create a schedule, recurring monitor, or competing maintenance queue as part of an ordinary invocation.
