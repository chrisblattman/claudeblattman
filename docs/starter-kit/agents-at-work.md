# Get ahead of the curve

**If you can't use agents at work, you should absolutely still learn to use them.** Pick something from your own life that you already want done. Plan a trip, sort household paperwork, compare an expensive purchase, or finally get a personal website off the ground. The practice should pay for itself in useful work.

You'll learn how to give an agent enough context, let it take a first pass, and work with it to improve the result. If your workplace later permits agents, you'll have experience to draw on. In the meantime, you might get a better vacation out of it.

Use your own device and personal material for these projects. If chatbots are all you're allowed to use at work, [there's advice for that below](#get-more-from-the-chatbots-you-can-use-at-work).

## Pick a project from your own life

Here are a few ideas: [plan a vacation](../workflows/life/vacation.md), [research a purchase](../workflows/life/shopping.md), [write something in your own voice](../build-your-own/voice.md), [keep up with school or club emails](../workflows/school-digest.md), [organize your tax paperwork](../tax-workflow/index.md), or [prepare for a health or fitness conversation](../workflows/life/health.md).

Choose one, or use any other personal project you'd like help with. Then work through **sections 1 and 2 of [the Essentials checklist](../index.md#my-absolute-essentials-and-where-to-start)** with that project: start using an AI app on your computer, then try councils and adversarial review.

**Ask your AI to walk you through it.** You don't need to learn the setup before you have a useful job for it. Start with something as simple as:

```text
I want help with [personal project]. Walk me through the first two sections of the Essentials checklist on claudeblattman.com, using this as my project. Help me get set up, ask what you need to know, and then let's start the work.
```

**The same best practices apply whether the project is personal or professional.** Give it a [project folder](../workflows/organize-projects.md), explain what you want and what matters to you, and [use a council](occasional.md) for independent approaches to the task. Then add your own judgment and revise. You know your family's preferences, budget, and constraints better than the agent does.

When you're ready to stop, ask it to save the decisions and next steps in the project. If you've installed the starter kit, `/done` saves a handoff for your next session. That way, you can keep developing the work without explaining everything again.

## Get more from the chatbots you can use at work

**If your workplace allows chatbots but prohibits agents, use the approved chatbot for your work tasks.** It can help you draft, edit, understand documents, compare approaches, and examine a plan. Give it material your workplace permits you to use with that tool.

The same conversational approach works here. Explain the job, give it the context, and ask it to help you think through the work:

```text
I need to [task] for [audience]. Here is the relevant background: [context]. Help me think through the approach, ask me the questions that would change your recommendation, and then take a first pass.
```

Go back and forth as many times as you need. Tell it what's missing, what it misunderstood, and what you'd do differently. You don't have to arrive with a polished prompt.
