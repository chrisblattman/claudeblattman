# Are you dictating instead of typing?

I basically never type anymore. Voice dictation—talking instead of typing—is the single biggest productivity change I've made alongside AI chatbots.

The useful change is how much context you give the agent. Talk through what you want, where the material is, what you've already tried, and what worries you. You don't need to turn those thoughts into a polished prompt before you begin.

**I use [Wispr Flow](https://wisprflow.ai/) across the apps where I write.** You can also start with the dictation already available in Claude or Codex.

## Get started

<a id="getting-started"></a>
<a id="try-dictation-for-free"></a>
### 1. Choose a dictation app

**You can start with the dictation built into Claude or Codex.** This is a good first step with no separate dictation subscription, but it does not let you dictate into other apps or windows on your computer.

**I recommend installing a separate dictation app.** Wispr Flow, Granola and Fireflies Talk are options for typing by voice across your apps:

- [Download Wispr Flow](https://wisprflow.ai/downloads) — Mac, Windows, iPhone and Android.
- [Download Granola](https://www.granola.ai/download) — its new [voice typing](https://www.granola.ai/voice-typing) is currently available on Mac (and coming soon to iOS).
- [Download Fireflies](https://fireflies.ai/desktop) — its desktop app includes Fireflies Talk for Mac and Windows.

All three offer both dictation and meeting transcription tools. Fireflies' meeting bot shows up as a separate participant in virtual meetings, which I find less elegant, though its [desktop app also offers recording without a bot](https://fireflies.ai/desktop).

### 2. Choose your keyboard shortcut

**Ask your AI to help you figure out the key bindings.**

```text
I want to use [app] for dictation on this computer. Can you walk me through the key bindings or help me choose the shortcut I want?
```

Try starting and stopping dictation with the shortcut, then dictate a short message. If you're using the built-in dictation in Claude or Codex, you can also use its dictation control in the message box.

## Use it on your work

<a id="why-dictation-changes-everything"></a>
<a id="how-i-use-it"></a>
### 3. Dictate a real task and your feedback

Start a session in your project folder. Explain the job as you would to a colleague: what needs to exist at the end, who it's for, where the useful files are, and what you haven't decided yet. For example:

```text
I need a slide deck for a funder. The earlier proposals and figures are in this project, and our correspondence is in Gmail. I'm still figuring out the story. I want to show what we've learned and what we'd do next, but I don't want to bury them in background. We have about twenty minutes with them. Help me work out the flow, and ask me what else you need to know.
```

**Give it the context, even if that takes several paragraphs.** Add details as they occur to you. The agent can organize the request and ask questions; you don't have to speak in a particular order. This is probably the biggest gain from dictation. I'm willing and able to give the agent much more context when I can speak to it rather than having to type it all out.

If you've installed the council skill, you can include “Use a council to help me with this” in the same message. The [council guide](../starter-kit/occasional.md) walks through developing and reviewing the work.

If there are terms the dictation tool continuously gets wrong, consider adding them to the app's personal dictionary. Wispr Flow calls this its [Dictionary](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/4052411709-teach-flow-your-words-with-the-dictionary).

**Dictate your feedback too.**

Read the result and talk through what you'd change. Say what to keep as well as what to revise:

```text
The opening spends too long on background. Start with the result the funder cares about. Keep the figure, but explain its takeaway before introducing the methods. The funding request needs to be clearer. Revise it and show me the new flow.
```

For longer pieces, I collect comments as I read and have the agent wait until I've finished. You can send several dictated messages without asking it to rewrite after each one. Start with:

```text
I'm going to give you comments in a few messages. Hold them until I say I'm finished. Don't start revising yet.
```

When you're ready:

```text
That's all my feedback. Bring the comments together and revise the draft.
```

Go back and forth until you're satisfied. If you've set up your [voice improvement system](../how-i-improve-the-work.md), you can then ask it to learn from your corrections.

<a id="what-wispr-flow-does"></a>
### 4. Use dictation in your other apps

Claude and Codex's built-in dictation works inside those apps. To take the same habit into email, documents and notes, use a dictation service that works across apps.

**Wispr Flow.** I use it all day, every day, and find it well worth paying for. Its free desktop plan includes a 2,000-word weekly allowance; unlimited dictation is **US$15 a month**. It also works on my iPhone (and I've since started dictating as I walk to work). See the [current plans and allowances](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/9559327591).

**Granola.** Its newly launched [voice typing](https://www.granola.ai/voice-typing) currently offers unlimited words for free, including on the Basic plan. It's available on Mac, with Windows, iOS and Android listed as coming soon.

**Fireflies Talk.** [Fireflies' desktop app](https://fireflies.ai/desktop) also offers free, unlimited dictation on every plan, including the free one, for Mac and Windows.

*Plans and availability checked October 2026.*
